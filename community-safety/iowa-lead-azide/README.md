# Middletown lead azide event: resident safety links

**Independent public-information guide · Last checked November 19, 2026**

The U.S. Army says it plans two controlled detonations of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant near Middletown on **Friday, November 20, at about noon Central Time**. The Army says the material is unsafe to move and that controlled detonation is the risk-reduction method. This is a planned operation, not a report of an attack on residents. It can cause a loud noise, ground vibration, and lead-containing dust.

> **The forecast and official directions can change. Check the Army update and local emergency-management notices before making decisions. This guide does not determine whether a particular address is affected and is not an evacuation order.**

## What to do

### If the Army has identified your home in the Middletown City Park neighborhood

The current Army advisory asks residents in the identified nearby homes to leave **8 a.m.–2 p.m. Central Time on November 20**, mainly because vibration could break windows. The Army lists a temporary place at the **Armed Forces Reserve Center, 16815 DMC Highway 34, Middletown**. The Army says family members and pets are welcome; keep pets secured in carriers or on leashes. Check the latest Army notice or call **563-505-4359** to confirm arrangements.

### If your location is within the current air-dispersion area

Follow the latest Army and local public-health instructions. The Army advises people in affected areas to avoid the area if possible; if indoors, close doors and windows, set heating/cooling to **recirculate** if that option is available, and avoid unnecessary travel through the area. Wait for the Army's all-clear before treating the exposure period as over.

### Prepare for household needs

- Charge phones and any needed mobility or communication devices; keep medicines, identification, keys, and essential supplies close.
- Plan support and transportation for children, older adults, disabled residents, and anyone who needs help leaving or staying indoors.
- Keep children and pets indoors and away from outdoor dust in an affected area. Wash hands before eating; remove shoes at the entrance to reduce tracking dust indoors.
- Do not dry-sweep or disturb dust. Wait for official cleanup instructions and use damp cleaning methods recommended by public-health authorities.

For possible exposure concerns—especially for a child under six—contact a clinician or local health department and ask whether blood-lead testing is appropriate. For immediate danger, call 911.
