# Middletown: what should residents do?

**A simple safety guide for the planned lead azide detonation**  
Last checked: November 19, 2026

## First: do you have to leave?

**The Army is not asking all of Iowa to move out.** It is asking residents in identified homes near Middletown City Park to be away from home on **Friday, November 20, from 8 a.m. to 2 p.m. Central Time**. The request is because vibration could break windows.

If your home is in or near that neighborhood and you are unsure whether the request applies to you, contact the Army at **563-505-4359** or check its latest notice.

The Army lists a temporary place to go at **Armed Forces Reserve Center, 16815 DMC Highway 34, Middletown**. It says family members and pets may come; pets should be secured in a carrier or on a leash.

## If you live elsewhere

Check the latest map and your local emergency alerts. The Army's current plan is for two controlled detonations at about **noon Central Time**. They may cause a loud bang, shaking, and dust that contains lead.

If your location is inside the current **air map** area, follow the latest official advice. The Army says to stay indoors, close windows and doors, set heating or cooling to **recirculate** if available, and avoid unnecessary travel through the area. Wait for the official all-clear.

**The map is a forecast, not a test result.** Its colored areas show where a computer model predicts dust could travel; they do not mean lead was measured at every home. Weather can change the forecast, so check for updates on Friday.

## A few things to prepare

- Keep phones, needed medicines, keys, and essential mobility or communication equipment close and charged.
- Make a plan for children, older adults, disabled people, pets, and anyone who needs help with travel or staying indoors.
- In an affected area, keep children and pets indoors. Wash hands before eating and take shoes off at the door to reduce tracking dust inside.
- Don't dry-sweep or stir up dust. Wait for official all-clear and cleanup advice. EPA recommends damp cleaning to reduce lead dust.
- If you are worried about possible exposure, especially for a child under six, ask a clinician or local health department whether a blood-lead test is appropriate. Call **911** for immediate danger.
