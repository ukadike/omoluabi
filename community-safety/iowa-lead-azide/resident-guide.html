Middletown lead azide detonation

This guide helps residents find the Army’s current maps and understand the official instructions. It is an independent companion, not an Army alert or a public-health order. Check the official update links below before acting; forecasts and instructions can change.

Planned for Friday, November 20

The Army currently schedules two controlled detonations at about noon Central Time at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant near Middletown. The operation may cause a loud noise, ground vibration, and a dust plume containing some lead.

Identified homes near Middletown City Park: the Army asks residents to leave from 8 a.m. to 2 p.m. Central Time, mainly because vibration could break windows. This temporary request applies to identified nearby homes, not all of Iowa.

Temporary place listed in the updated Army advisory: Armed Forces Reserve Center, 16815 DMC Highway 34, Middletown, IA. Access is controlled and limited to identified Middletown residents. Confirm details with the Army if you need assistance.

The EPA permit requires a forecast six hours before the operation, hourly updates from then until completion, and a final modeled impact estimate within six hours after the event. Check again Friday morning and follow any local alerts.

How to read the colors

Pink: modeled surface deposition of at least 5 µg/ft². Follow the indoor-air and travel precautions in the official notice.
Orange: modeled surface deposition of at least 100 µg/ft². The EPA permit adds wet-cleaning of high-contact surfaces after the official exposure period.
Cyan: modeled airborne-lead contour. It marks a conservative planning area, not a measured concentration at every location.

What a shaded area does not mean

  • It does not mean monitors have measured lead there.
  • It does not predict the same amount of lead at every point inside.
  • Its boundary is not a line between “safe” and “unsafe.”
  • It can move when wind and weather forecasts change.

What to do if your location is in the latest affected area

  1. Follow the newest Army map and your county or state health department’s incident-specific instructions.
  2. During the official exposure period, stay indoors if you cannot leave; close windows and exterior doors.
  3. If safe, set heating/cooling to recirculate and reduce outdoor-air intake. Avoid unnecessary travel through the mapped area.
  4. Wait for the Army’s all-clear or local public-health direction before resuming outdoor activities or cleaning.
  5. If your address is in the higher surface-deposition area, wet-clean high-contact surfaces as instructed. Keep children away until surfaces are cleaned. Do not dry-sweep suspected dust; EPA recommends damp-cloth cleaning for potential lead dust.

Additional guidance for potentially affected Illinois residents

Illinois Department of Public Health advises people who may be affected to stay indoors with windows closed for 24–48 hours after the detonation. It also advises handwashing, washing outdoor surfaces with soap and water, and removing shoes at the entrance to reduce dust tracked inside. Follow any newer local or state instructions.

Health and contact information

  • For health concerns or possible exposure, contact your clinician or local public-health department. Illinois IDPH advises asking a clinician about blood-lead testing if you are concerned that you or a child may have been exposed.
