# Correct the Map — Source Notes

Last reviewed: **2026-09-14**

This ledger separates the UN decision, cartographic properties, campaign claims, and Omoluabi’s editorial interpretation. It does not use a map projection as proof of a political outcome.

---

## 1. United Nations General Assembly — *Correct the Map*

**Source:** United Nations General Assembly, resolution *Correct the Map*, adopted 4 September 2026; text circulated as A/80/L.104 and subsequently issued as A/RES/80/307.

**Official record:** https://docs.un.org/en/A/RES/80/307
**UN meeting coverage:** https://press.un.org/en/2026/ga12801.doc.htm

### Supports

- The General Assembly adopted the resolution by a recorded vote of **164 in favour, 1 against, and 6 abstentions**.
- The resolution calls for wider use of equal-area map projections, particularly Equal Earth, where the comparative size of landmasses is relevant.
- The text recognizes that disproportionate cartographic representation can have cognitive, educational, cultural, geopolitical, and socioeconomic effects.

### Does not establish

- A legally binding ban on all Mercator maps.
- A single compulsory projection for all uses.
- That any particular curriculum, map application, news organization, or government has already changed its map practice.
- That use of Mercator independently caused a specified political or economic result.

## 2. African Union and the Correct the Map campaign

**Reporting:** Reuters, “Togo to ask UN member states to use map showing Africa’s true size,” 14 April 2026.
**URL:** https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/society-equity/togo-ask-un-member-states-use-map-showing-africas-true-size-2026-04-14/

### Supports

- The African Union tasked Togo with promoting the Correct the Map campaign internationally.
- Africa No Filter and Speak Up Africa led campaign advocacy for an equal-area alternative.
- The campaign proposed Equal Earth, a 2018 equal-area world projection.

### Does not establish

- That every African state uses one common map projection in all official materials.
- That campaign endorsement substitutes for the official UN resolution text.

## 3. Equal Earth projection

**Source:** Bojan Šavrič, Tom Patterson and Bernhard Jenny, “The Equal Earth map projection,” *International Journal of Geographical Information Science* (2019).

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/13658816.2018.1504949
**Reference site:** https://equal-earth.com/

### Supports

- Equal Earth is an equal-area pseudocylindrical world projection introduced in 2018.
- It preserves the relative areas of mapped regions.
- It was designed to offer an area-faithful world map with a familiar visual form.

### Does not establish

- Exact direction or distance across the whole map.
- Perfect local shape at every latitude.
- A general claim that every other projection is inappropriate.

---

## 4. Mercator projection and its intended use

**Source:** Encyclopaedia Britannica, “Mercator projection.”
**URL:** https://www.britannica.com/science/Mercator-projection

### Supports

- Gerardus Mercator developed the projection in 1569 for navigation.
- A constant compass course can be drawn as a straight line on the map.
- Scale inflation increases away from the equator, making high-latitude land areas look far larger than their relative area.

### Does not establish

- That Mercator is useless for every mapping task.
- That the original cartographer’s navigational purpose resolves later cultural or political use.

**Source:** Natural Earth, “Admin 0 – Countries,” 1:110m cultural vectors.

### Supports

### Does not establish

- A position on boundaries, country recognition, sovereignty, or disputed territories.
- Cartographic suitability for local or legal boundary work. The 1:110m scale is appropriate only for a global projection comparison.

## Editorial caution

“Africa looks smaller on Mercator” is a geometric fact about its projection. “That visual diminishment has contributed to the global collective imagination” is a historically and politically grounded interpretation recognized in the UN resolution and campaign argument, but it must not be inflated into an unmeasured claim that one map alone explains colonialism or present inequality.
