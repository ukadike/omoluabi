Evidence Machines: Investigating the Unexplained

How do we investigate something when the evidence is fragmented, multimodal, contested, incomplete, classified, badly described, or contradictory?

UAP / UFO records are the first stress-test corpus. They are not the conclusion.

The premise

Evidence Machines is a computational investigation studio built from the process used to create Omoluabi. Students begin with a difficult claim and build a public, inspectable path through its evidence rather than beginning with a predetermined answer.

The course treats computation as an investigative instrument: a way to search archives, align media, reconstruct timelines, expose contradictions, visualize uncertainty, and make a research process legible to other people.

Governing question

How do we know what we think we know when machines increasingly mediate the evidence?

This is not a UFO class

The UAP archive is unusually useful because it contains nearly every evidence problem at once: official records, testimony, photographs, video, sensor traces, missing data, redactions, conflicting interpretations, copied media, metadata problems, institutional uncertainty, and extraordinary public claims.

That makes it a strong first laboratory for learning how to investigate without collapsing uncertainty into belief or disbelief.

One investigation path

01 · Claim

State the question before the answer

Write the claim precisely enough that evidence can support, weaken, complicate, or leave it unresolved.

02 · Source discovery

Find the original record

Government archives, scientific literature, mission records, FOIA releases, databases, APIs, oral histories, and institutional repositories.

03 · Provenance

Ask where this came from

Who produced it? When? Is it original, edited, recompressed, quoted, or copied? Can another researcher retrieve it?

04 · Multimodal alignment

Put evidence into the same time and space

Align audio, transcript, photographs, video, maps, sensor states, weather, location, and event chronology where the record permits.

05 · Contradiction

Build the strongest counter-reading

Record evidence that supports the claim and evidence that complicates it. Do not hide explanatory alternatives.

06 · Protected uncertainty

Leave unresolved things unresolved

A responsible system needs a visible state for missing evidence, unresolved interpretation, and claims that exceed the public record.

07 · Public interface

Make the reasoning inspectable

Publish timelines, maps, evidence graphs, source ledgers, transcripts, media, accessible descriptions, and citations that let another person reconstruct the investigation.

Seven-week pilot studio

Week 01

The Claim

Choose a question strange or difficult enough that the answer is not obvious. Define what would count as evidence.

Week 02

Find the Original

Trace circulating claims backward into archives, records, datasets, media, and primary sources.

Week 03

Evidence Has a Body

Study what different media and sensors can actually register — and what they leave out.

Week 04

Machines as Investigative Partners

Use search, transcription, extraction, clustering, agents, and computational comparison while documenting hallucination and verification risk.

Week 05

Contradiction

Actively seek evidence against the working hypothesis and identify what remains genuinely unknown.

Week 06

Make the Investigation Visible

Prototype an evidence interface: map, graph, timeline, archive, sonification, spatial experience, or other computational form.

Week 07

The Public Record

Publish a reproducible investigation that another person can inspect, navigate, challenge, and continue.

What students make

The final artifact is not a conventional paper and is not graded on whether the original hypothesis turns out to be correct. It is an evidence machine: a computational interface that makes a difficult investigation inspectable.

Possible forms

  • Interactive evidence map
  • Source-provenance graph
  • Multimodal timeline
  • Audio / transcript alignment interface
  • Archive explorer
  • Sensor comparison instrument
  • Claim-mutation visualization
  • Accessible spatial or physical interface

Evaluation

  • Can another person reconstruct the research path?
  • Are primary and secondary sources distinguishable?
  • Are contradictions visible?
  • Is uncertainty represented rather than hidden?
  • Does the interface communicate what a sensor or source cannot establish?
  • Can disabled users inspect the same evidentiary logic?

Core assessment rule

The grade is not based on whether your hypothesis was right. It is based on whether another person can reconstruct how you reached it.

Accessibility is part of the evidence

Evidence changes when access changes. A video without an audio description, an audio record without a transcript, a map without a textual spatial equivalent, or a chart without its underlying data withholds part of the investigation from part of the public.

The studio therefore treats accessibility as a research requirement rather than a final formatting pass: keyboard navigation, semantic structure, transcripts, captions, alt text, plain-language summaries, nonvisual equivalents for spatial information, and accessible source trails are part of the investigative method.

Omoluabi as the reference implementation

Students do not have to reproduce Omoluabi. They use it as a working example of a larger proposition: computational systems can help people investigate difficult public evidence while keeping provenance, contradiction, consent, missingness, accessibility, and human editorial judgment visible.

The current UAP work demonstrates the method with official imagery, astronaut and witness records, mission chronology, source notes, and protected uncertainty. Future student investigations could examine disputed photographs, unidentified sounds, lost internet artifacts, historical mysteries, machine-learning anomalies, environmental events, or other contested records.
