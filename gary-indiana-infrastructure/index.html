Gary, Indiana: Civic Infrastructure Reliability

A source-led stress test of the systems a city depends on — and the dependencies those systems hide.

What you need to know

The August 11, 2026 storm did not produce only an electrical outage. The prolonged loss of power exposed dependencies across traffic signals, road access, fuel pumping, food and medication storage, home medical devices, schools, charging, communications, and emergency support.

Gary's Flock cameras and Real-Time Crime Center remain part of the investigation, but they are now one subsystem among many. Omoluabi's question is broader: which civic systems were resilient, which failed, how did failures cascade, and what redundancy existed for residents as well as institutions?

Next action

Build one shared timeline

Align feeder restoration, signal outages, road closures, fuel access, carrier/network conditions, water notices, hospital/911 continuity, school closures, shelters, transit, public-safety systems, and resident testimony on the same August 11–27 clock.

Map dependencies, not just outages

For each system record its power source, backup power, communications path, fuel dependence, physical-access dependence, failure time, restoration time, and who could still use it.

What the public record already shows

Electricity

A major, prolonged outage affected Gary; some homes and businesses waited nearly two weeks for restoration.

Traffic + roads

Large areas were reported without working traffic signals. The city documented extensive tree damage and blocked roads, with recovery still underway on August 18.

Fuel

CBS documented a Gary gas station whose pumps did not work; a generator kept part of the business alive, but the generator itself required fuel obtained elsewhere.

Medical continuity

Reporting documented difficulty managing medications and powered medical devices. Gary opened an oxygen-cylinder refill station for home-use patients affected by the outage.

Connectivity

AT&T/ITDRC and Verizon deployed charging/Wi-Fi resources. FirstNet deployed satellite communications support to the NIPSCO facility in Gary for utility recovery.

Schools

Gary public schools remained closed more than a week after the storm because of ongoing storm-related issues.

Water

Indiana American Water issued multiple Gary main-break/boil-advisory notices during the recovery period. The breaks are documented; storm causation is not established.

Shelter + charging

The Gary YWCA was listed as a 24/7 shelter and charging site; other food and emergency resources were distributed.

The cascade

Omoluabi treats infrastructure as a dependency graph. A failure can travel through systems that look unrelated:

ELECTRICITY
  → traffic signals → intersection safety / mobility
  → gas pumps → fuel → generators / vehicles / recovery
  → refrigeration → food + medication safety
  → home medical equipment → oxygen / mobility / health continuity

CELL-SITE POWER + BACKHAUL
  → mobile connectivity → emergency information / coordination

UTILITY COMMUNICATIONS
  → crew coordination → electric restoration

ROAD ACCESS
  → fuel / shelter / medical care / repair crews / transit

A generator without fuel access is not independent. A charged phone without network service is not connected. A shelter is not fully accessible if residents cannot physically reach it.

Infrastructure asymmetry — broadened

The investigation is no longer "did cameras stay on while homes went dark?" The stronger question is:

Which Gary systems had batteries, generators, satellite links, stored fuel, alternate routing, independent communications, or priority restoration — and which essential resident-facing systems did not?

Flock remains useful as one test case because the vendor describes solar/battery/cellular configurations. But the same analysis belongs on hospitals, utility communications, water/wastewater, police/fire facilities, traffic systems, cellular sites, transit, shelters, and other critical infrastructure.

Do not fill these gaps with assumptions

  • It is not established that the August storm caused the documented water-main breaks.
  • It is not established that Gary's wastewater treatment failed.
  • It is not established that Methodist Northlake lost power or clinical services.
  • It is not established that 911, CAD, police radio, or fire communications failed.
  • It is not established that GPTC fixed-route or paratransit service failed.
  • It is not established that Gary's Flock cameras or RTCC stayed online.
  • It is not established that government intentionally prioritized surveillance over residents.

Design question: civic redundancy

The Omoluabi design lesson is not simply to copy the power architecture of a surveillance camera. It is to design resident-facing redundancy as a network: neighborhood charging, medical-device support, accessible alerts, local/mesh communications, environmental sensing, outage and road observations, refrigeration for medications, and low-bandwidth/offline information.

A resilience node only works when it is connected to a plan for power + communications + mobility + water + medical access + food/fuel.
