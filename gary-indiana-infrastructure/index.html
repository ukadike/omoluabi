Gary, Indiana: Energy Reliability + Flock Infrastructure

A source-led investigation of disaster resilience: which systems failed, which systems may have independent power and communications, and what evidence is required before comparing them.

What you need to know

Gary experienced a severe and prolonged power disruption after the August 11, 2026 storm. Gary also operates a Real-Time Crime Center using surveillance and license-plate recognition, and official Lake County records document Gary Police Department's use of an extensive Flock camera array.

What is not yet known: whether Gary's Flock cameras or RTCC remained operational during the same outage period. Flock describes solar/battery and cellular capability, but product capability is not deployment-specific uptime evidence.

Next action

Get the device record

Request Flock inventory, model, power configuration, connectivity configuration, device-health / heartbeat telemetry, maintenance events, and RTCC system logs for August 11–25, 2026.

Build the outage timeline

Align NIPSCO feeder/circuit restoration with traffic signals, cellular service, emergency charging sites, the RTCC, and camera infrastructure. Compare like-for-like timestamps instead of general restoration percentages.

Why

Normal electric reliability and disaster resilience are different questions. A subsystem can survive a grid failure because it has a different power source, battery, generator, communications path, or restoration priority. Omoluabi therefore tests infrastructure asymmetry rather than assuming it.

Investigation question: Did any public-safety surveillance nodes maintain autonomous power and communications while nearby residential or civic systems were unavailable — and, if so, what design and policy choices explain the difference?

Design question

If independently powered and connected sensing nodes prove more resilient than surrounding civic infrastructure, that architecture becomes a design precedent — not an endorsement of surveillance.

A resident-governed resilience node could adapt solar/battery power and independent communications for emergency information, accessible alerts, phone and assistive-device charging, environmental sensing, outage observations, and local communications, with transparent ownership, data minimization, and community governance.

Omoluabi rule

A powered camera is not necessarily connected. A connected camera is not necessarily accessible to the RTCC. A vendor capability is not a deployment fact. And the survival of one subsystem does not prove another was intentionally deprived.
