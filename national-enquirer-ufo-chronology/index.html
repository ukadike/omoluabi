National Enquirer UFO coverage

An issue-by-issue research prototype for tracing how UFO claims were framed, sourced, disputed, and repeated in The National Enquirer.

Coverage status: This is a starter corpus, not a verified complete account of every UFO-related item. A clipping archive is described as containing a couple hundred Enquirer articles from 1961–1988. This prototype currently indexes selected items for method development. Absence from this list does not mean an issue had no UFO coverage.

Omoluabi rule: AI assists. Rules govern. Humans publish. A published headline is evidence that a claim was printed; it is not, by itself, evidence that the event happened.

Images and archival traces

Images are evidence objects with their own provenance and reuse status. The first image is a public-domain U.S. federal photograph; the clipping entry is linked as an archival lead because its reuse permissions are not established.

Citizen and witness reports

These are people’s accounts as attributed in contemporary coverage or preserved in records. The page distinguishes civilian observers, pilots, and a military helicopter crew. A report is testimony about an experience; it does not by itself identify what caused it.

How the engine reads this archive

Timeline

Issue publication date and reported-event date occupy separate fields. Unknown event dates remain blank.

Source ladder

Each item links to the material supporting its catalog entry and labels that source type. Secondary indexes are not presented as issue scans.

Contradiction

Disputed attributions, competing publication dates, and unsupported causal claims remain visible as unresolved records.

Entries describe what a cited source says about Enquirer coverage. They do not certify UFO claims. This demonstration contains no automated truth score and makes no extraterrestrial-origin determination.
