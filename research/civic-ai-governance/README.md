# Omoluabi / Civic AI Governance

**Research branch**  
**Status:** Canonical  
**Published:** 17 September 2026

## Core question

Frontier AI companies and prominent technology leaders increasingly describe advanced AI as carrying potentially severe societal or catastrophic risks. Those warnings may reflect genuine safety concerns, commercial incentives, regulatory strategy, institutional responsibility, or combinations of these factors.

Omoluabi will not assume the answer.

The research question is:

> **When frontier-AI companies describe AI as extraordinarily powerful and potentially dangerous, how does that rhetoric interact with their commercial interests, regulation, market concentration and control of compute?**

The proposition that AI-risk rhetoric can also function as marketing is treated here as a **testable hypothesis**, not a conclusion.

## Investigation frame

Compare:

- what companies and executives publicly say about AI risk;
- what systems they release and on what timetable;
- how rapidly they scale compute;
- what regulation they support, oppose or seek to shape;
- how safety commitments change over time;
- how much independent evaluation and oversight they permit;
- who can access frontier compute;
- where capital expenditure and infrastructure are concentrated;
- what countries and institutions control the underlying stack.

The purpose is to separate **documented safety practice, public-interest governance, commercial positioning, regulatory strategy and rhetoric** wherever the evidence permits.

## The African governance question

The branch will not begin with:

> How should Africa regulate American AI?

It will begin with:

> **What institutions should govern African AI infrastructure that Africans themselves own, operate or substantially control?**

This opens a different research agenda: public compute, community ownership, African-language models, local data trusts, mineral provenance, energy rights, accessibility, model transparency, procurement, research access, environmental accounting and citizen appeal.

## Shared concept: Compute Sovereignty

This branch joins AEN / African Sovereign Compute through the Small Systems Lab concept of **Compute Sovereignty**:

**Energy + Materials + Hardware + Compute + Data + Models + Governance**

A country can possess critical minerals while retaining little control over processing or high-value manufacturing. It can possess data while renting the compute. It can deploy local-language models while depending on externally controlled infrastructure.

Compute Sovereignty asks where meaningful agency must exist across the stack for communities, institutions and states to make durable choices about their technological future.

## Governance research areas

### 1. Frontier-AI alarm
Create a dated evidence base of public risk statements, safety frameworks and policy positions from major AI companies and technology leaders.

### 2. Safety vs. commercial incentives
Compare stated safety positions with releases, investment, compute expansion, lobbying, partnerships and market strategy.

### 3. Compute concentration
Map ownership and control of chips, hyperscale cloud, data centers, model infrastructure and access.

### 4. African governance frameworks
Compare the African Union Continental AI Strategy, national AI strategies, data-protection regimes, procurement rules and regional institutions.

### 5. Mineral and compute sovereignty
Trace the relationship between resource extraction, processing, component production, hardware, cloud infrastructure and model access.

### 6. Public-interest AI
Investigate public, university, cooperative, municipal, nonprofit and commons-based compute models.

### 7. Rights and accountability
Develop governance proposals around accessibility, due process, auditability, environmental accounting, public procurement, community consent and redress.

## Method

Omoluabi will distinguish:

- **fact** — directly supported by a reliable source;
- **claim** — attributed to the person or institution making it;
- **hypothesis** — a proposition being tested;
- **inference** — a conclusion derived from multiple pieces of evidence;
- **unknown** — information not established by available evidence.

The branch should preserve disagreement and contradictions rather than flattening them into a single narrative.

## Relationship to AEN

AEN asks how African-controlled computational infrastructure could be physically designed, powered and built.

Civic AI Governance asks who should own it, how it should be governed, what rights people retain, and what public obligations accompany control of compute.

> **AEN builds the physical possibility. Omoluabi investigates the civic conditions.**
