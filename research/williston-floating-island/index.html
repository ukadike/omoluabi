Williston Reservoir floating island

A real-world stress test for source ladders, map missingness, movement traces, provenance, and visible uncertainty.

Omoluabi home · Proof of Method · Research record · GeoJSON map anchors

What you need to know

Determination: The floating vegetated mass is real and its relocation is verified. Its exact formation site, age, release mechanism, and route between observations remain unresolved.

Core finding: not seeing the object at its previous location did not establish that it had disappeared. The correct state was LOCATION_UNKNOWN_WITHIN_SEARCH_AREA.

Case summary

Object

Approx. 140 m × 70 m, about 9,800 m², according to BC Hydro.

First documented area

West of Finlay Bay; earliest known satellite image cited by BC Hydro: July 21, 2026.

Later documented area

Along the Ospika Arm shoreline; satellite image dated August 15 and location confirmed August 31.

Reported displacement

Approximately 30 km northwest, according to BC Hydro. The exact intervening track is not public.

Timeline integrity

  1. July 21, 2026 — earliest known satellite imagery cited by BC Hydro captures the island west of Finlay Bay.
  2. August 13 — BC Hydro says it receives reports, photographs, and video from local boaters.
  3. Mid-August — the object is no longer publicly located at the first documented area.
  4. August 15 — satellite imagery later reviewed by BC Hydro shows the mass near the Ospika Arm.
  5. August 31 — BC Hydro confirms the location along the Ospika Arm.
  6. September 2 — BC Hydro publishes its update.

Flag: TIMELINE_INCOMPLETE. BC Hydro also describes the movement as approximately 30 km “over 15 days.” The public timeline does not identify which 15-day interval that phrase refers to. Omoluabi preserves the discrepancy rather than inventing a reconciliation.

Historical-context test

BC Hydro's 2021 Williston Reservoir Wetland Habitat Monitoring report includes a Floating Island (FI) habitat class describing large persistent floating masses of organic matter, coarse woody debris, and mineral soil with vegetation capable of becoming established. That context changes the narrative probability of the general phenomenon without proving the origin of this specific 2026 mass.

Next action

The highest-value evidence is not another speculative explanation. It is a georeferenced observation set: exact satellite footprints/timestamps, island polygons for each confirmed observation, reservoir elevation, wind and current data, historical imagery backtracking, and remote structural documentation. BC Hydro warns that the mass is unstable, so an Omoluabi field workflow should be remote-sensing first.
