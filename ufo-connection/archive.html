The Witness Record

A claim changes when we can place the witness beside the recording, the recording beside the instrument, and the instrument beside the event. This archive preserves those layers without forcing them into a single explanation.

Evidence first. Attribution always. Uncertainty stays visible.

01Primary record

Mission audio, official film, government files, contemporary transcripts and archival catalog records.

02Witness layer

What a named witness said, with the date of the statement kept separate from the event date.

03Contradiction

Where records disagree, both versions remain visible until another source can test them.

ARCHIVE METHOD

No retroactive certainty.

A later memory is not silently promoted into a contemporaneous record. An official explanation is preserved as an institutional finding, not treated as omniscient. “Unidentified” records a limit in identification; it does not identify an extraterrestrial origin. Every media item below links back to a source of record and includes a text alternative or transcript note.

DOSSIER 01 · GORDON COOPER / EDWARDS AFB

One witness record, several different evidentiary eras.

Cooper's public record spans military service, the documented Edwards photographic case, a cautious 1962 television interview, real-time Mercury mission audio, a 1978 letter advocating scientific investigation, and much later accounts of a landing at Edwards.

  1. 1951 · LATER TESTIMONY

    European formation sightings

    Cooper later said that he and other pilots saw repeated formations of unidentified objects while he was stationed in Europe. A contemporaneous squadron or radar record has not yet been added to this archive.

  2. MAY 1957 · ARCHIVAL CASE

    Edwards AFB photographs

    The National Archives film inventory identifies Edwards AFB material by Gettys & Bittick as two 35mm black-and-white negative filmstrips. The archive date and later McDonald date conflict by one day.

  3. NOV 1962 · PRIMARY AUDIOVISUAL

    Cronkite interviews Cooper

    Cooper says qualified observers have reported objects without logical explanations, while explicitly allowing that individual cases may ultimately have explanations.

  4. MAY 1963 · MISSION AUDIO

    Faith 7 “fireflies” and particles

    Cooper reports luminous particles during Mercury-Atlas 9. NASA's released descriptions connect one sequence to frozen condensation and another to mission-related xenon-strobe beacons.

  5. JULY 1968 · CONGRESSIONAL RECORD

    McDonald reconstructs Edwards

    Physicist James E. McDonald tells a House committee that he interviewed camera operators Bittick and Gettys and supervisor Frank Baker. His reconstruction describes an object photographed while moving away, but does not describe touchdown or landing legs.

  6. NOV 1978 · SIGNED STATEMENT

    Cooper writes to Grenada's UN mission

    Cooper advocates coordinated scientific collection of UFO reports and states his belief that extraterrestrial vehicles visit Earth. The letter documents Cooper's position; it is not independent corroboration of extraterrestrial origin.

  7. LATER INTERVIEWS · MEMORY LAYER

    The Edwards story becomes a landing

    Cooper later describes landing gear, touchdown and a close approach. That version is materially stronger than the earlier Bittick/Gettys reconstruction and remains stored as a separate testimony layer.

CONTRADICTION MAP · EDWARDS 1957

The archive does not currently support one seamless Edwards story.

  • DATE

    May 2 or May 3?

    NARA's film inventory gives May 2, 1957. McDonald's 1968 congressional statement calls it May 3. Original camera/range logs and film metadata are the next test.

  • LANDING

    Did it touch down?

    McDonald's interviews with Bittick and Gettys describe an object photographed as it moved away. Cooper's later account adds landing legs, touchdown and close approach.

  • IDENTIFICATION

    Weather balloon?

    The Air Force/Blue Book record reportedly identified a balloon. McDonald recorded that the camera operators rejected the explanation. A geometric reconstruction requires the balloon track, winds, timestamps and camera angles.

  • FILM CHAIN

    What survives?

    NARA catalogs two 35mm negative filmstrips. Gettys later remembered roughly 30 exposures. The highest-generation surviving negatives must be inspected before making claims about image detail.

FORENSIC RECONSTRUCTION · EDWARDS AFB 1957

The film exists. The measurement chain is the investigation.

The archival question is no longer whether the Edwards event left records. NARA catalogs two 35mm negative black-and-white filmstrips under Gettys & Bittick, and a derivative Project Blue Book case cover identifies File 4715 with 41 photos. The Air Force record card carries a balloon conclusion. Omoluabi is now testing whether the surviving measurements reproduce that conclusion.

  1. PRIMARY ARCHIVE · VERIFIED

    NARA item 1003460

    May 2, 1957 · Edwards AFB · Gettys & Bittick · two 35mm negative black-and-white filmstrips.

  2. CASE FILE · VERIFIED IDENTIFIER

    NAID 28970018 / T1206 Roll 27

    The NARA-hosted case-file scan set contains 106 pages. Its Project 10073 record card marks the case as a confirmed balloon and directs the reader to the case file.

  3. DERIVATIVE CASE COVER

    File 4715 · 41 photos

    A Blue Book derivative scan visibly identifies File 4715, May 2, 1957, Edwards AFB, California, and “41 photos.” This must be reconciled with NARA's two filmstrips and Gettys's later recollection of roughly 30 exposures.

  4. IDENTIFIER CONFLICT

    4715 versus 4750

    A separate public-domain Project Blue Book PDF is labeled Case 4750 Photos, USA for Edwards on the same date. We are keeping those identifiers separate until their NARA roll relationship is established.

  5. GEOMETRY · VERIFICATION GATE

    Secondary numbers do not yet cohere

    Published secondary frame angles, coordinates and a claimed balloon altitude become internally inconsistent when treated as simultaneous measurements. That is a retrieval diagnostic—not evidence against the balloon conclusion. Each number must now be traced to an original camera or balloon record.

  6. COOPER CONNECTION

    No contemporaneous Cooper document found yet

    The earlier Edwards witness reconstruction does not contain Cooper's later landing sequence. We are searching specifically for a 1957 Cooper memo, routing slip, report, signature or chain-of-custody entry.

DOSSIER 02 · APOLLO ANOMALY LEDGER

What astronauts reported, what the instruments recorded, and what remained unidentified.

The Apollo archive is treated as a mission reconstruction problem: astronaut speech, onboard audio, technical debriefs, photography, spacecraft state, trajectory, lunar geography and later interpretation should be synchronized before any origin claim is made.

  1. APOLLO 10

    “Outer-space-type music”

    The crew reported an unusual whistling sound in lunar orbit. Young proposed VHF ranging and Stafford a side lobe; during a later recurrence the sound ceased immediately after rendezvous-radar breakers were opened. Read the evidence note.

  2. APOLLO 11 · TRANSLUNAR

    Unidentified bright object

    Armstrong, Aldrin and Collins discussed a bright object in the technical debrief. The reported S-IVB distance argued against that candidate; the crew did not establish the object's range or size, and spacecraft-origin debris was not excluded.

  3. APOLLO 11 · LUNAR ORBIT

    Aristarchus brightness

    Mission Control asked the crew to inspect Aristarchus after reports of transient lunar activity. The crew described unusual brightness in the region. The record supports an observation; it does not by itself establish its cause.

  4. APOLLO 17 · Cislunar

    Bright drifting fragments

    Cernan, Schmitt and Evans described multiple bright angular or jagged fragments. In the same record they considered paint and ice, including material associated with the separated S-IVB.

ACCESSIBILITY + PROVENANCE

Every new item must be usable without sight or sound.

  1. AttributionPublisher, archive, record identifier, event date and statement date stay attached to the item.
  2. Alt / audio descriptionImages and visual evidence receive non-interpretive descriptions of what is visibly present.
  3. TranscriptAudio and video receive a transcript or clearly labeled editorial transcript summary, with speaker and timestamp information as it becomes available.
  4. ProvenanceCopies, scans, derivatives, missing originals and chain-of-custody gaps are stated rather than hidden.
  5. Data alternativeTimelines and evidence relationships are mirrored in machine-readable JSON so the interface is not the only way to inspect the archive.

Governing principle: The archive can show that an observation occurred without pretending that the archive knows what caused it.
