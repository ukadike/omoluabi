## Apollo 11 — translunar unidentified object

The July 31, 1969 technical crew debrief records Armstrong, Aldrin and Collins discussing a bright object seen during translunar flight. The crew considered the Saturn S-IVB stage, contacted the ground, and were told it was thousands of miles away. The object's range and size were not established, and the debrief does not rule out spacecraft-origin debris.

Primary technical debrief:
https://sma.nasa.gov/SignificantIncidents/assets/a11_tcdb.pdf

NASA Apollo 11 transcript hub:
https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a11/a11trans.html

**Accessibility description:** Text record of three crew members comparing the appearance of a distant bright object through unaided vision, monocular and sextant optics. The record contains no resolved range measurement or close-up image of the object.

## Apollo 11 — Aristarchus

Houston advised Apollo 11 that transient lunar events had been reported near Aristarchus and asked the crew to observe the area. The air-to-ground transcript records the crew searching the region and commenting on unusual brightness.

Primary air-to-ground transcript:
https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/as11-tec.pdf?emrc=340ba7

**Accessibility description:** Voice-transcript sequence in which Mission Control directs the crew toward Aristarchus using lunar coordinates and the crew visually inspects the area in earthshine.

## Apollo 17 — cislunar fragments

NASA-UAP-D009 preserves an Apollo 17 audio sequence in which Gene Cernan, Harrison Schmitt and Ronald Evans report small bright particles/fragments outside the spacecraft. They describe some as jagged/angular and discuss paint or ice chips, including material associated with the separated S-IVB.

Official DVIDS release:
https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1007872/nasa-uap-d009-apollo-17-audio-excerpt-december-7-1972

**Accessibility description:** Audio-only archival mission communications. The astronauts verbally describe bright drifting fragments; there is no corresponding close-up visual record in the released DVIDS item.
