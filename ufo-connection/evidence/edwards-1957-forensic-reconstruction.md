# Omoluabi Forensic Reconstruction — Edwards AFB, May 1957

**Status:** Active investigation  
**Updated:** 24 September 2026  
**Case:** Project Blue Book 4715 / Edwards AFB  
**Rule:** Primary archive, institutional conclusion, witness testimony, later recollection, and secondary reconstruction remain separate.

## Current evidentiary position

The Edwards event is no longer treated here as a story about "missing UFO film."

The archival trail is real and specific:

- A Project Blue Book derivative scan is labeled **File 4715 · May 2, 1957 · Edwards AFB, California · 41 photos**.
- The National Archives audiovisual transfer inventory lists **item 1003460**, dated **May 2, 1957**, observer/source **Gettys & Bittick**, described as **two 35mm negative black-and-white filmstrips**.
- The National Archives identifies the Edwards textual case file as **NAID 28970018**, within **T1206 Roll 27**; a derivative interface exposes 106 NARA-hosted scans.
- The Project 10073 record card in the case file marks the conclusion/comment as **confirmed balloon / see case file**.
- A 1968 congressional hearing contains James E. McDonald's later reconstruction after he interviewed James D. Bittick, John R. Gettys Jr., and supervisor Frank E. Baker.
- McDonald's reconstruction describes an object being photographed and then moving away into the distance. It does **not** document Cooper's later landing-gear/touchdown sequence.

This establishes a genuine photographed Air Force case with surviving archival material. It does not establish an extraterrestrial craft.

## Official balloon conclusion

The case-file record card visibly carries a balloon conclusion. A period reproduction of a June 11, 1957 Edwards Air Force Base information-office letter likewise says the Air Force considered the object a weather balloon and referred to independent tracking, bearings, elevations, speed, and the morning weather-balloon release.

Omoluabi therefore records:

**Official institutional conclusion:** balloon.

That conclusion is not erased because later witnesses disagreed with it.

The next question is narrower and testable: **do the surviving measurements reproduce that conclusion?**

## Witness reconstruction — McDonald, 1968

McDonald's statement to the House Committee on Science and Astronautics reports that he interviewed Bittick, Gettys, and Baker. His account says the camera operators wanted a second camera station for triangulation, but another station was not staffed. They therefore photographed from one station.

McDonald records the witnesses describing a golden, domed object, with differing memories of apparent features around the dome, and an object that eventually moved away into the distance.

Important limitation:

**This reconstruction contains no landing, no three landing legs, and no close approach to a landed vehicle.**

Official hearing record:
https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/CHRG-90hhrg97818

## Geometry reconstruction: verification gate

Secondary researchers publish specific phototheodolite and balloon values, including possible frames **609, 614, 620, 651**, object angles near **azimuth 10°28′ / elevation 2°24′** shifting toward **40°30′ / 1°00′**, an observation point around **34°53′30″N, 117°40′30″W**, and a weather-balloon launch around **34°54′30″N, 117°52′00″W**.

These values have **not yet been independently matched to the original camera log or film-edge data**. They are therefore research inputs, not established facts.

### Preliminary consistency test — secondary inputs only

Using those published secondary coordinates:

- straight-line ground distance between the proposed observation point and proposed launch point: **~17.58 km**
- initial bearing from proposed observation point toward proposed launch point: **~276.1° (west-northwest)**

A secondary account also claims the balloon was around **39,300 ft MSL** at approximately 08:15. If Edwards ground elevation is approximated at about **702 m MSL**, the vertical separation is about **11.28 km**.

At that vertical separation:
- an elevation angle of **2.4°** implies a horizontal slant geometry on the order of **269 km**
- an elevation angle of **1.0°** implies a horizontal distance on the order of **646 km**

Those distances are plainly incompatible with a balloon only tens of kilometres away **if all of the secondary values refer to the same instant, datum, camera station, and target**.

This is **not a finding that the Air Force balloon explanation is false**. It is a finding that the secondary numbers, taken literally together, are internally inconsistent and therefore must be traced to their primary pages before use.

Possible causes include:
- wrong time assignment;
- wrong camera/station coordinate;
- MSL versus AGL confusion;
- azimuth/elevation belonging to a different frame or target;
- balloon altitude copied from a different time;
- transcription error in later secondary literature.

The simulation is therefore a **retrieval diagnostic**, not an identification.

## Weather-data retrieval target

The historical Edwards upper-air station is identified in NOAA/IEM archives as **WMO 723810 / KEDW**. The IEM radiosonde archive is backfilled from NOAA/NCEI IGRA data and can contain balloon bearing/range fields when those survive.

## Current conclusion

The strongest case-supported statement is:

> **Professional Edwards range-camera personnel photographed an object on May 2, 1957; Project Blue Book officially classified it as a balloon; film material is cataloged at NARA; later witness testimony disputed the explanation; Cooper's much later landing story is not corroborated by the earlier reconstruction reviewed so far.**

That is the baseline the next evidence must change—not a presumption of alien craft and not a presumption that every later discrepancy is meaningless.
