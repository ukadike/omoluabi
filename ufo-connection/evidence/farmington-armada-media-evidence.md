# Farmington Armada — Newspaper + Media Evidence Packet

**Omoluabi evidence archive · Packet 01**  
**Event window:** March 15–17, 1950  
**Primary location:** Farmington, New Mexico, United States  
**Published:** September 30, 2026  
**Status:** Living evidence record

> **Working conclusion:** The surviving record strongly supports that a major, widely observed aerial event was reported in Farmington in March 1950 and received immediate local, regional, Associated Press, and United Press coverage. The record does **not** establish what the objects were or that they were extraterrestrial craft.

## Evidence rule

This packet separates:

1. evidence that an observation/reporting event occurred;
2. evidence about what witnesses said they observed;
3. proposed explanations;
4. later testimony;
5. claims that remain unsupported or unresolved.

Document authenticity is not the same as claim authenticity. A real newspaper, government memo, or interview can preserve a claim without proving the claim's underlying explanation.

## 3. Contradiction card — how many objects?

The object count is **not stable across sources**.

- The March 18 *Farmington Daily Times* report says estimates ranged from “several” to more than 500.
- The contemporaneous United Press story says Boddy and a group of businessmen believed they saw more than 100.
- In James E. McDonald’s retrospective investigation roughly seventeen years later, Boddy estimated that he personally observed about 20–30 disc-shaped objects.

**Omoluabi finding:** Do not publish “500 UFOs were seen” as a settled count.

Preferred language:

> Contemporary estimates varied dramatically, from tens to more than one hundred and, in one local report, approximately 500.

This is a classic case where **the observation event is better supported than the precise quantity reported**.

## 4. Retrospective witness layer — James E. McDonald

Atmospheric physicist James E. McDonald investigated the Farmington case in the late 1960s.

In his prepared statement for the 1968 U.S. House symposium on unidentified flying objects, McDonald described Farmington as a multiple-witness, multiple-object case and explicitly acknowledged the limitation that his interviews occurred roughly seventeen years after the event.

## 5. Cotton / airborne-material explanation

The 1950 local reporting preserves a dispute over whether at least some observations involved cotton-like material carried in the air.

The press attributed such an explanation to State Patrolman Andy Andrews. Harold Thatcher rejected the idea that the objects he was tracking were cotton, saying he could distinguish nearby floating material from the objects he was attempting to observe.

This explanation must remain in the record because it is **contemporary counter-evidence**, not an inconvenience to be removed.

### Unresolved question

Did different observers see different things at different moments — e.g. airborne material during one observation and a different phenomenon during another?

The existing press record is not sufficient to resolve that question.

---

## 6. Weather/time-context caution

The March 18 local story includes both references to high wind/dust limiting visibility and descriptions associated with clearer conditions.

These statements may refer to different observation periods across the multi-day event. Omoluabi should not merge them into a single weather state without reconstructing:

- exact observation date;
- exact observation time;
- witness location;
- weather-station data;
- wind direction/speed by hour;
- cloud cover/ceiling;
- visibility;
- astronomical conditions.

## 7. Photograph provenance — excluded from verified core

A photograph circulates in later UFO research showing six apparent objects in a V-shaped arrangement, accompanied by handwriting associating it with Farmington, March 17, 1950.

At present this packet does **not** treat it as verified photographic evidence of the Farmington event because the following remain unresolved:

- original photographer;
- original negative;
- first publication;
- custody chain;
- contemporaneous authentication;
- independent metadata.

**Status:** lead only / provenance unresolved.

## 9. Current Omoluabi assessment

### Strongly supported

- A major aerial-observation event was publicly reported in Farmington during March 1950.
- The event generated immediate and geographically distributed newspaper coverage.
- Numerous witnesses were identified by name in contemporary reporting.
- Witness accounts included recurring features, including multiple objects and at least one red object.
- Contemporary accounts disagree substantially about numbers and interpretation.
- Later researchers re-interviewed several named witnesses.

### Not established

- Exact object count.
- Exact altitude or speed estimates.
- That every witness observed the same phenomenon.
- That the observed objects were technological craft.
- That the observed objects were extraterrestrial.
- A demonstrated connection to the alleged 1948 Aztec crash.
- A demonstrated connection to later recovered-craft allegations.
- Provenance of the circulating Farmington V-formation photograph.

## Editorial rule

**Observation ≠ explanation.**

Omoluabi can say that Farmington produced a significant, contemporaneously documented mass-reporting event. It cannot yet say what the objects were.

New evidence should be appended with dates and provenance. Contradictions should remain visible rather than silently reconciled.
