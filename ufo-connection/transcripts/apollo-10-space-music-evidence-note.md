# Apollo 10 — “space music” evidence note

**Mission:** Apollo 10  
**Crew:** Thomas Stafford, John Young, Eugene Cernan  
**Event:** Whistling / “outer-space” music during lunar-orbit rendezvous operations  
**Primary transcript:** NASA Apollo 10 Lunar Module onboard voice transcript  
**NASA source:** https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/as10-lm.pdf?emrc=1d39a1  
**Synchronized Flight Journal:** https://apollojournals.org/afj/ap10fj/as10-day5-pt20.html

## Transcript status

This is an evidence note built from the NASA onboard voice transcript and the
Apollo Flight Journal synchronization. It is not a substitute for the complete
mission recording.

## What was heard

At approximately mission elapsed time 102:13, Cernan calls attention to an
unusual whistling sound. Stafford and Young hear it as well. Cernan describes
it with the short phrase “outer-space-type music.”

The sound continues intermittently while the Lunar Module *Snoopy* and Command
Module *Charlie Brown* are separated and conducting rendezvous/ranging work.

## What the crew themselves proposed

The contemporaneous conversation contains technical hypotheses rather than an
external-object interpretation:

- Young suggests the sound is probably associated with **VHF ranging**.
- Stafford suggests a **side lobe**.
- Later, while preparing the rendezvous sequence, Stafford opens the rendezvous
  radar circuit breakers. Cernan immediately remarks that it has become quiet.

The temporal relationship is important evidence, but it should not be promoted
into stronger causation than the record supports without a radio/radar-system
reconstruction.

## Omoluabi reading

**Observed/heard:** all three crew members acknowledge the unusual whistling.

**Contemporaneous hypotheses:** VHF/ranging/radar effects are proposed by the
crew themselves.

**Not present in the record:** a visual craft, an external transmitter, or a
crew claim that the sound came from extraterrestrial beings.

**Next test:** synchronize the DSEA audio with LM/CSM VHF and rendezvous-radar
configuration changes and determine whether the tone begins/ends with the
relevant hardware states.

## Accessibility description

Audio/transcript event involving three male crew voices inside separated Apollo
spacecraft. The evidentiary content is an intermittent whistling sound and the
crew's spoken discussion of possible onboard communications/radar causes.
