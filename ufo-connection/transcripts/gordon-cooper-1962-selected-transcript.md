## Transcript summary

Walter Cronkite asks Cooper whether he still thinks there may be something to reports of unidentified flying objects.

Cooper answers that a substantial number of highly qualified observers have reported objects for which they did not have a logical explanation. He immediately adds an important qualification: it remains possible that each report ultimately has an explanation.

The conversation then moves to the possibility of planets with conditions that could support life. Cooper says he considers it plausible that other planets could possess a livable atmosphere and some form of life.

## Omoluabi reading

The 1962 recording establishes **Cooper's public position at that date**. It does not establish the origin of any particular object.

What the record supports:
- Cooper considered some witness reports genuinely unexplained.
- Cooper regarded some of the witnesses as highly qualified.
- Cooper did not say that unexplained automatically meant extraterrestrial.
- Cooper entertained the possibility of life elsewhere.

What the record does not establish:
- A specific craft or encounter.
- A technological origin.
- Extraterrestrial visitation.
- That later versions of Cooper's Edwards AFB story were already part of his account in 1962.
